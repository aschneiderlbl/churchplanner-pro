Volunteer scheduling

A Simpler Way to Schedule Church Volunteers With ChurchPlannerPro

ChurchPlannerPro lets volunteers see the schedule, pick up open shifts, and swap or drop shifts when their plans change. Your coordinator publishes the shifts that need to be filled. Volunteers handle their own signups and changes without asking the coordinator to arrange each one.

1. Your coordinator publishes the shifts

Start with the volunteer roles your church needs covered, such as greeters, ushers, or coffee hosts. Your coordinator publishes those shifts in ChurchPlannerPro so volunteers can see where help is needed.

The schedule gives volunteers a place to choose when they can serve.

2. Volunteers pick up open shifts themselves

Volunteers open the member app, look at the available shifts in their groups, and pick up the ones that work for them. They can also see their upcoming shifts, so they know when they’re serving.

The coordinator doesn’t have to collect everyone’s choices and add each name to the schedule.

3. Volunteers swap or drop shifts when plans change

If a volunteer can no longer serve, they have two options:

  • Swap a shift with another volunteer.
  • Drop a shift so someone else can pick it up.

A dropped shift becomes available for another volunteer to claim. The volunteers can make these changes themselves, without asking the coordinator to find a replacement or arrange the exchange.

For example, suppose a greeter picks up a Sunday shift and later realizes they’ll be out of town. They drop the shift in ChurchPlannerPro. Another volunteer sees the opening and picks it up. The coordinator doesn’t need to pass messages between them or enter the replacement.

4. Your coordinator can see what still needs help

Your coordinator can review the schedule to see which shifts are filled and which are still open. If nobody picks up an opening, they can follow up on that specific need.

That leaves fewer changes for the coordinator to manage. Volunteers can choose their shifts and work out changes themselves, while the coordinator keeps an eye on the schedule.
