ChurchPlannerProvs.Planning Center

A Planning Center alternative for pastors who don’t have an admin

It’s Thursday afternoon. One of Sunday’s greeters texts, “I can’t make it this week.” If you pastor a small church without an administrator, that usually means you’re the one who checks the schedule, asks around for a replacement, and lets everyone know who’s covering Sunday.

With ChurchPlannerPro, the greeter can reply to Church Assistant. The assistant checks the backup list you’ve set up and asks people in order until someone says yes. Then it lets both volunteers know. If the spot is still open, you hear about it. On Sunday morning, one text tells you who’s serving and where you still need a hand.

That’s what it looks like to run your church by text. You can ask “Who’s greeting on Sunday?” in your staff group chat and get an answer from the church schedule. Your volunteers can confirm, decline, or ask for a swap in their own words. Even the usher who still carries a flip phone can participate.

Both platforms cover the everyday work of running a church, including people, groups, volunteers, giving, events, registrations, check-in, and communication. Planning Center gives ministry leaders connected tools for each area. ChurchPlannerPro is built for the pastor who carries several of those responsibilities personally and wants to hand off routine work with a text.

How a week works when you’re the pastor and the scheduler

Start with a schedule you already have. If your church uses Planning Center, you can connect it to Church Assistant and try text coordination with one team. Your greeters are a good place to start. You know who usually serves, who’s happy to fill in, and which service they attend.

  • Thursday. Church Assistant texts each scheduled volunteer. A reply like “Yes, see you Sunday” confirms the spot. If someone declines, the assistant works through your backup list.
  • Saturday evening. Volunteers get a reminder with their role, arrival time, and where to check in. The person who agreed to cover has the details they need.
  • Sunday morning. You get a summary of who’s serving and any openings. If something unusual comes up, like a volunteer calling in sick at 7 a.m., the assistant brings it to you.

You still decide who’s a good fit for the nursery and when a volunteer deserves a personal call. What you can hand off is the round of texts it takes to learn that everyone is covered.

The rest of the church week

Sunday school and VBS. A parent asks after worship whether there’s still room for her second grader. ChurchPlannerPro keeps registration, class groups, room assignments, attendance, and parent communication together. You can check the program record for the child’s place. If the class needs another helper, that’s a scheduling question you can bring straight to Church Assistant.

Members and communication. Some questions belong in the staff chat, like who’s opening the building. Others need a personal response, like a family asking about baptism. ChurchPlannerPro gives you member and family records, forms, groups, a calendar, and email so the next step has a place to live after the conversation ends.

Giving and finances. Your treasurer still needs to reconcile the month, prepare giving statements, and explain how spending compares with the budget. During setup, we review the records and financial tools your church already uses. Your treasurer can show us how a gift moves from Sunday’s offering to the monthly report, so the setup reflects the work they actually do.

You don’t need to hire an admin to get started

Changing church software can sound like a job for the very person you don’t have. Someone has to clean up the old directory, work out which volunteer list is current, and make sure the right people get the first reminder.

ChurchPlannerPro includes hands-on setup. Bring the schedule you use today, even if it’s a spreadsheet one volunteer maintains. We’ll help you review the records, connect the tools you want to keep, and set up the first team. If you’d also like a person to help with recurring office work, optional part-time administrative support is available separately.

Which one fits your church?

Planning Center is a good fit when your ministry leaders want specialized tools and have people who use them each week. If your worship leader builds the order of service in Services and your musicians depend on its charts and rehearsal materials, that’s a concrete reason to keep it. You can still connect Planning Center to Church Assistant if volunteer follow-up is the part landing on you.

ChurchPlannerPro is built for you if you’re the pastor people text whenever a shift opens, a schedule changes, or nobody knows who’s on nursery. You get a church management system for the wider work of the congregation and an assistant you can reach from the conversation you’re already in.

Try it with the next Sunday on your calendar.
