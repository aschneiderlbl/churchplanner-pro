How we protect your church’s data Security

Our production environment is hosted on AWS EC2 and protected with multiple layers of security. ChurchPlannerPro is designed with security as a foundational principle.

Network

  • The application is delivered through a Cloudflare Tunnel, so the server is not exposed directly to the public internet.
  • AWS firewall rules (Security Groups) are configured to restrict inbound access.
  • SSH access is limited to explicitly allowed sources.
  • Application and database ports are bound to localhost and are not publicly accessible.

Access management

  • Administrative access to the server is controlled through SSH key authentication and operating-system permissions.
  • Cloudflare Tunnel configuration is restricted to authorized Cloudflare accounts.
  • Application secrets (API keys, database credentials) are stored outside of container images and protected with restricted file permissions.

Threat protection

  • Cloudflare provides edge protection, including DDoS mitigation.
  • Additional controls such as rate limiting and firewall rules can be applied.
  • The server is monitored and can be configured with alerts for availability and health.

Information protection

  • Data in transit is encrypted using TLS/HTTPS via Cloudflare.
  • The tunnel connection between Cloudflare and the server is encrypted.
  • Data at rest is stored on encrypted AWS storage volumes.

Backups

  • Nightly backups of the database can be produced and retained for a defined period.
  • Backups can optionally be copied to an external storage location for added resilience.

Planning Center alternative The Planning Center alternative built around how your church actually works

Planning Center gives churches a powerful collection of products. ChurchPlannerPro takes a different approach: start with the work your church is trying to accomplish and bring everything required to do it together.

ChurchPlannerPro · 10 min read

Sign up

Planning Center is one of the best-known church management platforms available. It gives churches tools for managing people, services, giving, groups, registrations, check-ins, calendars and more.

For many churches, that flexibility is exactly what they need.

But it also reflects a particular way of thinking about church management software: give churches a collection of specialized products and let them configure those products around the way their church operates.

ChurchPlannerPro takes a different approach.

We believe church staff shouldn’t have to become software experts to make their church management system work.

Instead of starting with software modules, ChurchPlannerPro starts with the work your church is actually trying to accomplish.

  • Schedule volunteers
  • Run Sunday school
  • Reconcile giving
  • Communicate with members
  • Manage events

Then we bring the people, data, tools and workflows required to accomplish that work together.

That’s the fundamental difference between ChurchPlannerPro and Planning Center.

At a glance

Both platforms can help churches manage many of the same day-to-day operations. The difference is largely in how those capabilities are organized and how much work your church wants to do to configure them.

The problem with organizing church software around features

Open almost any church management platform and you’ll see a familiar list: people, groups, calendars, registrations, check-ins, services, forms and giving.

Those are all important capabilities. But they’re not the work your church is trying to accomplish.

A church administrator doesn’t come into work thinking, “Today I need to use People, Groups, Registrations and Calendar.”

They’re thinking:

  • “I need to get Sunday school ready for this weekend.”
  • “I still need three volunteers for the 10:30 service.”
  • “I need to reconcile Sunday’s donations.”
  • “I need to get the newsletter out by Thursday.”

ChurchPlannerPro is designed around the outcome, not the collection of features required to produce it.

The same idea applies to giving

A church doesn’t really need another financial dashboard.

It needs to know whether donations were recorded correctly, whether the systems agree, whether deposits reconcile and whether the treasurer can get the reports they need.

Churches often have giving information spread across multiple systems. That can leave staff manually comparing reports, fixing discrepancies and moving information from one system to another.

ChurchPlannerPro is designed to bring that information together and make the reconciliation the focus.

The goal isn’t more financial software. It’s getting the financial work done.

In fairness

Planning Center’s modular approach has real advantages

There are good reasons Planning Center is organized the way it is.

A large church may have different teams responsible for worship services, children’s ministry, giving, groups and communications. Those teams may want specialized products with deep configuration options.

Planning Center also has a mature ecosystem that has been built over many years.

If your church already has a Planning Center setup that works well, your staff knows how to use it and you rely heavily on specific Planning Center functionality or integrations, switching systems may not make sense.

ChurchPlannerPro isn’t trying to recreate Planning Center one module at a time.

A different premise

For many churches, simplicity is more valuable than configurability.

Your church shouldn’t have to be the systems integrator

Software is rarely the only system a church uses.

You might have a giving platform you like, an accounting system your treasurer depends on, a website platform, a background-check provider or a tool used by a specific ministry.

And maybe a few spreadsheets that have somehow become mission-critical over the years.

Changing church management systems shouldn’t require replacing all of those things overnight.

ChurchPlannerPro is designed to connect with the tools your church already uses.

  • Keep what works.
  • Replace what doesn’t.
  • Move over time.

That allows us to bring information together, clean up the underlying data and reduce the amount of manual work required to keep different systems synchronized.

You shouldn’t have to implement church software by yourself

Buying church software is the easy part.

Then someone has to migrate member records, clean up duplicates, fix outdated information, configure the new system, connect other software, recreate processes, train staff and get members using it.

For a large church with dedicated operations or technology staff, that may be manageable.

For a smaller church, it can be a major project.

ChurchPlannerPro takes a hands-on approach to implementation.

We’ll learn how your church currently operates, understand the systems you’re using, help migrate and clean your data, connect the tools you want to keep and configure ChurchPlannerPro around the work your church needs to accomplish.

Hands-on implementation

You don’t have to figure everything out yourself.

Sometimes software isn’t actually the problem

We’ve also realized something else while talking with churches.

Sometimes the problem isn’t that the church has the wrong software.

There simply aren’t enough hours in the week.

The pastor is updating member records. Someone is chasing volunteers. A staff member is manually preparing the newsletter. The treasurer is reconciling information between systems.

Better software can make those jobs easier.

But someone still has to do the work.

That’s why ChurchPlannerPro can also pair the software with a part-time church administrator who works alongside your staff.

The administrator can help keep member information current, manage calendars and registrations, coordinate volunteer schedules, prepare communications, manage forms and follow-up, assist with reconciliation and keep different church systems synchronized.

The software makes the work more efficient. The administrator helps get the work done.

Deciding

So, is ChurchPlannerPro a good Planning Center alternative?

It depends on what you’re looking for.

Planning Center may be the better fit if

  • You want a mature ecosystem of specialized church products.
  • You have staff who want extensive configuration options.
  • Your existing Planning Center setup already works well.
  • You depend on specific Planning Center capabilities or integrations.
  • You prefer assembling workflows across specialized products.

ChurchPlannerPro may be the better fit if

  • You want one system organized around church operations.
  • You want fewer products and workflows to configure.
  • You want volunteer pickup, swaps and drops built into the member experience.
  • You want to manage programs like Sunday school as a complete workflow.
  • You want to connect existing systems instead of replacing everything immediately.
  • You need help migrating and cleaning up church data.
  • You want hands-on implementation.
  • You want the option to add administrative help.

Neither approach is right for every church.

But we think a lot of churches have spent years adapting their processes to the way church management software works.

We’re interested in doing the opposite.

Data quality

Four duplicate families you almost certainly have.

9 September 2026 · 1 min read

Every church database we have imported has had duplicates in it. Not because anyone was careless — because a family joined twice, once when they started attending and once when their child was registered for Sunday school, and nothing ever reconciled the two records.

The same person, entered twice

The commonest pattern is a maiden name and a married name on separate records, both active, both receiving statements. The second commonest is a nickname — Bob and Robert, Kathy and Katherine — with different email addresses.

In a 300-member database, four to twelve duplicate families is normal. Most administrators know about one or two of them.

The audit report flags all of them on import, with the evidence for each match, and you decide what merges.

ChurchPlannerPro runs a data health check on everything it imports, so you see the duplicates before you rely on the data.
